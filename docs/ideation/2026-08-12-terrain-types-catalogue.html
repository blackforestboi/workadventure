Grounding Context

WorkAdventure maps are orthogonal 32×32-pixel tile maps. They support transparent tiles for material transitions, multiple visual layers, and a per-tile collides flag. That supports a palette in which a shoreline, cliff edge, or hazardous ground is visually distinct and mechanically tagged without confusing it with a prop.

Terminology: terrain is a navigable surface; a prop is a discrete object placed on it (tree, boulder, bench, crate, fence); a biome is a themed combination of terrain, props, lighting, weather, and sound (for example, “haunted swamp”).

Ranked Ideas

These are ordered by the most generally useful sets to create first, not by aesthetic preference. Entries are deliberately surfaces, not scenery.

Traversal tags: W walkable, S slow, B blocked, SW swimmable, CL climbable or one-way slope, H hazardous, C conditional, I interactive/scriptable.

7. Reusable states, transitions, and mechanics

State overlays: lush; dry; damp; wet/puddled; muddy; flooded; frosted; snow-dusted; snow-covered; ice-coated; leaf-covered; overgrown; burned/charred; contaminated; cracked; rubble-strewn.

Transition tiles: create edges, corners, and directional variants for shorelines, wet-to-dry ground, sand-to-grass, path-to-ground, rock-to-soil, cliffs, walls, and bridges. These are variants of their adjoining surfaces, not new terrain families.

Axis
All axes
Basis
direct: Tile transparency supports visual transitions; tile collision is an explicit per-tile property.
Rationale
Composability controls asset growth and makes a single palette work across seasons and story states.
Downsides
Requires a small, documented tag vocabulary and careful editor UX.

Rejection Summary

Considered typeDecision
Forest, jungle, desert, tundra, city, cave, ruinsBiome/palette, not a terrain type; build each from surface, props, ambience, and overlays.
Trees, bushes, flowers, crops, mushrooms, roots, boulders, crates, fences, buildings, vehiclesProps or structures; use collision, layering, and interaction data where needed.
Snowy grass, wet grass, burned grass, mossy stone, cracked concreteState/detail overlays on a base terrain, unless their mechanics differ materially.
Slippery, damaging, slow, swimmable, climbable, one-way, conveyor/current, opaque, blockedTraversal/mechanics tags, not terrain variants.
Walls, doors, stairs, rail tracks, bridgesStructures unless the floor tile itself determines passage; bridges and stairs additionally require explicit elevation/layer behavior.
